# Day 1 of solving PhD qualifying exam problem(s): Harvard University Qualifying Exams II (Fall 2012) Q5

**Question.** Consider the principle ideal 

$$
I = (y^2 - x^3 + x)
$$

in the polynomial algebra $A = \mathbb{Q}[x,y]$, and let $R := A/I$.

(a) Show that the ring automorphism  $\tilde \sigma$ of  $A$ that sends  $x$ to  $x$ and  $y$ to  $-y$ descends to an automorphism  $\sigma$ of  $R$.

(b) For any associative and commutative ring  $S$ with unit and any unit al ring automorphism  $\tau$ of  $S$, show that the image of a principle ideal (resp. a prime ideal) of  $S$ under  $\tau$ is still a principle ideal (resp. A prime ideal). 

(c) Show that the ideal  $\mathfrak p = (\overline x, \overline y)R$ generated by the image of  $x$ and that of  $y$ is a prime ideal.

(d) Show that while  $\mathfrak p^2$ is a principle ideal,  $\mathfrak p$ is not. (*Hint.* Use (b) and the fact that $\mathfrak p$ is left invariant by $\sigma$.)

**Answer.** 

(a) Let $f := y^2 - x^3 + x$. $\tilde \sigma$ fixes $I$, i.e., $\tilde \sigma(I) = I$ (and hence $\tilde \sigma^{-1} (r) \in I$ for all $r \in I$) because

$$
\tilde \sigma(I) = \tilde \sigma(Rf) = \tilde \sigma(R) \tilde \sigma(f) = (\tilde \sigma(f)) = (f) = I.
$$

Therefore, it descends an automorphism at quotient ring level. Explicitly, define

$$
\sigma : R \to R, r + I \mapsto \tilde \sigma(r) + I.
$$

This map is trivially a surjective homomorphism.

To see that it is injective, let 

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \sigma(r + I) = 0 + I\\
\implies & \tilde \sigma(r) + I = 0 + I\\
\implies & \tilde \sigma (r) \in I\\
\implies & \tilde \sigma^{-1}(\tilde \sigma (r)) = r \in I\\
\implies & r + I = 0 + I,
\end{aligned}
$$

implying $\sigma$ is injective. Therefore, $\tilde \sigma$ descends an automorphism $\sigma$ of $R$.

(b) 

Let $(g) \subset S$ be a principle ideal. Then 

$$
\tau((g)) = \tilde \sigma(Rg) = \tau(R) \tau(g) = (\tau(g)),
$$

which is again a principle ideal.

Now, let $\mathfrak p \subset S$ be a prime ideal. 

Let $ab \in \tau(\mathfrak p)$. So there exists $e \in \mathfrak p$ such that $\tau(e) = ab$ We want to show that $\tau(\mathfrak p)$ is prime, i.e., $a \in \tau(\mathfrak p)$ or $b \in \tau(\mathfrak p)$.

Since $\tau$ is an automorphism, there exists $c, d \in S$ such that $a = \tau(c)$ and $b = \tau(d)$. So

$$
\tau(e) = ab = \tau (c) \tau(d) = \tau(cd) \implies e = cd \implies cd \in \mathfrak{p},
$$

which implies that $c \in \mathfrak p$ or $d \in \mathfrak p$, i.e., $a \in \tau( \mathfrak p)$ or $b \in \tau (\mathfrak p)$.

(c) Since $I \subseteq (x, y) \subseteq A$, using [Third Isomorphism Theorem for rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_theorems#Theorem_D_(rings)) we get

$$
R / (\overline x, \overline y) = (\mathbb{Q}[x,y] / I) / ((x, y)/I) \cong \mathbb{Q}[x,y]/(x,y) \cong \mathbb{Q}.
$$

Therefore, $\mathfrak p$ is a maximal ideal, hence, prime ideal.
