# Limits and colimits

Goal of this blog post is to ultimately define the concept of *limits* and *colimits* in category theory. This blog post closely follows this YouTube [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e_kL--6YZUU).

To define limits and colimits, we need the concept of cones and cocones respectively. And to define cones and cocones, we need to rigorously define the concept of a diagram in a category.

## Diagram

**Definition (Diagram).** A $\mathcal J$**-shaped diagram** in a category $\mathcal C$ is a functor 

$$
D: \mathcal J \to D.
$$

Here, $\mathcal J$ is called **index category**.

Intuitively, one can think of $\mathcal J$ as specifying what portion/pattern of $\mathcal C$ you want to look at and how its objects are related. A good mental picture is as follows:

## Limit & colimit

Now that we've defined the notion of diagrams, cones and cocones, we can finally define limits.

**Definition (Limit).** A **limit of the diagram** $D: \mathcal J \to \mathcal C$ is defined to be the terminal object in the category of cones of $D$. We denote it by $\varprojlim D$.

**Definition (Colimit).** A **colimit of the diagram** $D: \mathcal J \to \mathcal C$ is defined to be the initial object in the category of cocones of $D$. We denote it by $\underset{\longrightarrow}{\operatorname{colim}}$.
